# Comprehensive Form E Guidance

A complete guide to completing Form E (Financial Statement for Financial Remedy Proceedings) in divorce/dissolution cases in England and Wales.

*Compiled from: [AdviceNow](https://www.advicenow.org.uk), [Cripps Solicitors](https://www.cripps.co.uk), [Wikivorce](https://divorce.wikivorce.com), [Sharp Family Law](https://www.sharpfamilylaw.com), [Gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk)*

---

## Overview

### What is Form E?

Form E is a sworn financial statement required in financial remedy proceedings during divorce or dissolution of a civil partnership. It provides a comprehensive snapshot of your financial position and is used by both parties and the court to make fair decisions about dividing assets and arranging financial support.

### When is Form E Required?

- **Mandatory** when going through the courts for financial remedy proceedings
- **Commonly used** in mediation and solicitor negotiations (even outside court)
- **Not required** if you and your ex-spouse agree finances privately without court involvement (though still recommended)

### Key Facts

- **Length**: Approximately 28-30 pages
- **Time to complete**: 8-12 hours minimum (gathering documents takes longer)
- **Deadline**: Must be filed at least 35 days before your First Directions Appointment (FDA)
- **Time allowed**: Typically 12-16 weeks from court order to submission
- **Professional cost**: £750-£1,500 + VAT for solicitor assistance (3-4 hours)

### The Golden Rule

**Full and frank disclosure** - You have a legal duty to disclose ALL financial information truthfully. The form ends with a Statement of Truth. False or misleading statements can result in:
- Perjury charges
- The court setting aside any financial order
- Adverse costs orders
- Your credibility being destroyed

## Section-by-Section Guidance

## Section 1: General Information

This section establishes the basic facts about you, your marriage/partnership, and your family.

### 1.1-1.4: Personal Details

**What's needed:**
- Full name (including all middle names exactly as on official documents)
- Date of birth
- Current occupation (be specific - "accountant" not just "finance")
- Current address

**Tips:**
- Use your name as it appears on your marriage certificate
- Occupation should reflect your actual current role, even if between jobs

### 1.5-1.6: Marriage/Partnership Dates

**What's needed:**
- Date of marriage/civil partnership
- Date of separation
- Date petition was issued
- Dates of decree nisi and decree absolute (if applicable)

**Tips:**
- Separation date matters legally - it's when you stopped living as a couple, even if still under the same roof
- If you disagree on separation date, state your version and note the disagreement

### 1.7-1.9: Current Relationship Status

**What's needed:**
- Whether you've remarried or formed a new civil partnership
- Whether you're currently cohabiting with a new partner
- Whether you intend to cohabit within the next 6 months

**Tips:**
- Be honest about cohabitation - it affects financial claims
- "Cohabiting" means living together as a couple, not just sharing accommodation

### 1.10: Children of the Family

**What's needed:**
- Full names and dates of birth of ALL children of the family
- Who each child lives with
- The nature of your relationship to each child

**Important:** "Children of the family" includes:
- Your biological children together
- Children from previous relationships who were treated as part of the family
- Adopted children
- Step-children who lived with you as family

**Tips:**
- Include adult children if they're still financially dependent
- Be precise about living arrangements (e.g., "lives with mother, sees father every other weekend")

### 1.11: Health

**What's needed:**
- Any long-term physical or mental health conditions affecting you
- Any conditions affecting the children
- Only conditions relevant to earning capacity or financial needs

**Tips:**
- Focus on conditions that impact your ability to work or increase your expenses
- You'll need medical evidence to support claims
- Don't exaggerate - but don't minimise genuine conditions

### 1.12: Education

**What's needed:**
- Current school/educational arrangements for children
- Future planned arrangements (e.g., secondary school, university)

**Tips:**
- Include details of school fees if applicable
- Mention any special educational needs

### 1.13: Child Maintenance

**What's needed:**
- Current maintenance arrangements (CMS/CSA, private agreement, or none)
- Amount being paid and by whom
- If no arrangement, what you think should be paid

**Tips:**
- Use the CMS calculator to check if private arrangements are reasonable
- If through CMS, state whether it's "Direct Pay" or "Collect and Pay"

### 1.14-1.15: Other Court Proceedings

**What's needed:**
- Details of ANY other court proceedings between you (past or present)
- This includes: domestic abuse orders, children proceedings, property disputes, bankruptcy

**Tips:**
- Include proceedings that have concluded - the court wants the full picture
- Don't hide unflattering proceedings - non-disclosure is worse

### 1.16: Current Housing

**What's needed:**
- Where you currently live
- On what basis (owner, tenant, living with family, etc.)
- If renting, the rent amount and tenancy terms

## Section 2: Financial Details

This is the largest and most complex section. **Accuracy is critical.**

### Document Requirements (Grey Boxes)

Each page has a grey box listing required documents. **These are not optional.** Common requirements:
- 12 months bank statements for ALL accounts
- Property valuations (within last 6 months)
- Mortgage redemption statements
- Pension CETV statements (within last 12 months)
- 12 months payslips and latest P60
- Last 2 years tax returns (if self-employed)
- Business accounts (last 2 years)

**Start gathering these immediately** - pensions can take 3 months to provide CETVs.

### 2.1: The Family Home

**What's needed:**
- Full address
- Land Registry title number (find at gov.uk Land Registry for £3)
- Mortgage lender and type of mortgage
- Ownership structure (sole/joint names, tenants in common percentages)
- Current property valuation
- Outstanding mortgage balance
- Early repayment charges
- Estimated sale costs (typically 2-3% for agent, £1,500 for conveyancing)

**Calculating equity:**
```
Property value                    £400,000
Less mortgage                    -£150,000
Less early repayment charge       -£2,000
Less sale costs (3%)             -£12,000
= Net equity                      £236,000
Your interest (if 50%)           £118,000
```

**Tips:**
- Get 2-3 estate agent valuations and use the average
- If you disagree on value, state your figure and note disagreement
- If ownership is disputed, explain in the notes section

### 2.2: Other Properties

Same structure as 2.1 for each additional property:
- Buy-to-let properties
- Holiday homes
- Properties abroad (get professional valuation, note currency)
- Land

### 2.3: Bank Accounts

**What's needed for EACH account:**
- Bank name and branch
- Account type (current, savings, ISA)
- Last 4 digits of account number
- Whether sole or joint
- Current balance (from most recent statement)
- Your share if joint (usually 50%)

**Critical:**
- Include ALL accounts, even ones with small balances
- Include accounts you've closed in the last 12 months
- Include children's accounts if you have access/control
- Include overseas accounts

**Tips:**
- Unexplained large transactions will be questioned
- Consistent patterns of cash withdrawals look suspicious
- The other party will scrutinise your statements

### 2.4: Investments

**What's needed:**
- Institution name
- Type (stocks & shares ISA, bonds, unit trusts, direct shareholdings, cryptocurrency)
- Current value
- Joint or sole ownership
- Your share

**Tips:**
- Get current valuations, not historic purchase prices
- Include premium bonds
- Include crypto assets (get screenshots of wallet balances)
- Include shares in your employer if you have share options

### 2.5: Life Insurance

**What's needed:**
- Insurance company
- Policy type (term, whole life, endowment)
- Policy number
- Surrender value (not death benefit)
- Maturity date if applicable

**Tips:**
- Term life insurance usually has no surrender value
- Endowment policies often have significant value
- Contact each insurer for current surrender value

### 2.6: Monies Owed to You

**What's needed:**
- Who owes you money
- Why
- How much

**Examples:**
- Loans to family/friends
- Deposits paid (e.g., rental deposit)
- Tax refunds due
- Compensation claims pending

**Do NOT include:** Director's loan accounts (these go in Section 2.11)

### 2.7: Cash Holdings

**What's needed:**
- Cash held exceeding £500 total
- Where it's held
- What currency

**Tips:**
- Be honest - "I keep £2,000 in the safe at home" is fine
- Foreign currency should be converted to GBP at current rate

### 2.8: Personal Property Over £500

**What's needed:**
- Description of valuable items
- Estimated current value (what you'd get if sold today)

**Common items:**
- Vehicles (use Auto Trader for valuation)
- Jewellery
- Art and antiques
- Furniture (rarely worth much secondhand)
- Collections (stamps, coins, wine)
- Electronics

**Tips:**
- Don't overvalue your items or undervalue your spouse's
- Wedding/engagement rings - include but note sentimental value
- Inherited items - note this as the source

### 2.9: Liabilities (Debts)

**What's needed for EACH debt:**
- Creditor name
- Type of debt (credit card, loan, overdraft, HP)
- Whether sole or joint
- Total outstanding amount
- Your share of liability

**Include:**
- Credit cards
- Personal loans
- Overdrafts
- Car finance (HP/PCP)
- Store cards
- Money owed to family
- Student loans
- Tax owed

**Tips:**
- Include debts run up since separation
- Note if debts were for family expenses vs personal spending
- Be prepared to explain large debts

### 2.10: Capital Gains Tax

**What's needed:**
- Assets that would trigger CGT if sold
- Estimated CGT liability

**Tips:**
- Main residence usually exempt (Principal Private Residence relief)
- Buy-to-let properties will have CGT
- Get accountant advice if complex
- Transfers between spouses during marriage are CGT-free

### 2.11: Business Interests

**What's needed:**
- Business name
- What the business does
- Type (sole trader, partnership, limited company, LLP)
- Your ownership percentage
- Financial year end
- Whether latest accounts reflect current position
- Amounts the business owes you (director's loan)
- Business valuation
- CGT if business sold

**Documents required:**
- Last 2 years accounts
- Last 2 years tax returns
- Management accounts if year-end accounts are old

**Tips:**
- Business valuation is complex - may need forensic accountant
- Goodwill can be significant
- If accounts don't reflect reality, explain why
- **Flag for professional advice if business is significant**

### 2.12: Directorships

**What's needed:**
- Directorships held in last 12 months (other than businesses in 2.11)
- Even non-executive or unpaid directorships

### 2.13: Pensions

**What's needed for EACH pension:**
- Provider name
- Type (defined benefit/final salary, defined contribution/money purchase, state additional)
- Reference number
- Cash Equivalent Transfer Value (CETV)
- Date of CETV (must be within 12 months)
- Whether in payment
- If in payment, how much and how often

**Critical:**
- **Request CETVs immediately** - providers have up to 3 months to respond
- Basic State Pension doesn't need CETV
- Additional State Pension (SERPS/S2P) does need valuation

**Tips:**
- Pensions are often the largest asset after the house
- CETVs for defined benefit schemes can be surprisingly high
- Pension sharing orders are complex - get advice

### 2.14-2.19: Income

**What's needed:**

**Employment income:**
- Employer name
- Job title
- Gross annual salary
- Net monthly pay
- Bonuses (annual amount)
- Benefits in kind (car, health insurance value)

**Self-employment:**
- Business name
- Net profit last full year
- Drawings taken
- Estimated current year

**Investment income:**
- Dividends
- Interest
- Rental income (gross and net)

**State benefits:**
- Universal Credit
- Child Benefit
- Tax Credits
- Disability benefits
- Other

**Other income:**
- Maintenance received
- Trust income
- Family support

**Documents required:**
- 12 months payslips
- P60 for last tax year
- Tax returns (self-employed)
- Benefit statements

### 2.20: Summary

This is a totals page. Add up all your assets and liabilities:

```
Assets:
Property equity         £XX
Bank accounts           £XX
Investments             £XX
Life insurance          £XX
Personal property       £XX
Business interests      £XX
Pensions (CETV)         £XX
TOTAL ASSETS            £XX

Less liabilities        £XX

NET ASSETS              £XX
```

## Section 3: Financial Requirements

This section is about what you **need**, not what you **want**.

### 3.1: Income Needs (Monthly Budget)

You need to provide a detailed monthly budget. Be realistic - courts see many of these and will spot inflated figures.

**Housing costs:**
- Mortgage/rent
- Council tax
- Gas
- Electricity
- Water
- Buildings insurance
- Contents insurance
- Repairs/maintenance
- Ground rent/service charge

**Transport:**
- Car finance/lease
- Car insurance
- Fuel
- Maintenance/MOT/repairs
- Tax
- Parking
- Public transport

**Living expenses:**
- Food/groceries
- Household items
- Clothing
- Toiletries/haircuts
- Dry cleaning

**Children's expenses (if applicable):**
- School fees
- Childcare
- School uniforms/equipment
- Activities/clubs
- Pocket money

**Health:**
- Prescriptions
- Dental
- Optical
- Private health insurance
- Gym membership

**Communication:**
- Mobile phone
- Home broadband/phone
- TV licence
- Streaming subscriptions

**Leisure:**
- Holidays
- Entertainment
- Hobbies
- Eating out

**Financial:**
- Pension contributions
- Life insurance
- Savings
- Debt repayments

**Other:**
- Pet costs
- Gifts (Christmas, birthdays)
- Professional subscriptions

**Tips:**
- Use actual bank statements to calculate realistic figures
- Don't include one-off expenses in monthly figures
- Note which expenses are for you vs children
- Courts expect reasonable adjustments post-separation

### 3.2: Capital Needs

**What's needed:**
- Housing needs (type of property, location, rough cost)
- Furniture/furnishings if setting up new home
- Car if needed
- Any other capital requirements

**Tips:**
- It's acceptable to put "suitable housing - £TBC"
- Be realistic about housing - you may not maintain the same standard
- Furniture needs are usually modest unless starting from nothing

## Section 4: Other Information

This is your narrative section - your chance to explain context.

### 4.1: Significant Changes

**What's needed:**
- Changes to assets or income in last 12 months
- Anticipated future changes

**Examples:**
- "I was made redundant in March 2024"
- "I sold shares worth £15,000 to pay legal fees"
- "I expect to receive an inheritance of approximately £50,000"
- "My bonus is likely to be lower this year due to company performance"

### 4.2: Contributions

**What's needed:**
- Contributions you made to the family's assets/welfare
- Both financial and non-financial

**Examples:**
- "I gave up my career to raise our children"
- "I used £50,000 inheritance as deposit on family home"
- "I built the business that is now valued at £500,000"
- "I was primary carer for the children throughout the marriage"

### 4.3: Standard of Living

**What's needed:**
- Description of your standard of living during the marriage

**This matters because** the court considers whether that standard can be maintained.

**Tips:**
- Be factual, not aspirational
- Include holidays, cars, schools, lifestyle
- Note if standard of living was unsustainable (funded by debt)

### 4.4: Conduct

**What's needed:**
- Only conduct that would be inequitable to disregard

**Important:** Normal divorce behaviour is NOT relevant. The court doesn't care about:
- Adultery
- Who left whom
- General bad behaviour

**Conduct IS relevant for:**
- Deliberate dissipation of assets
- Domestic abuse (especially financial abuse)
- Hiding assets
- Excessive gambling/spending
- Litigation misconduct

**Tips:**
- Think very carefully before including conduct allegations
- Get legal advice first
- False allegations will damage your credibility

### 4.5: Other Information

**What's needed:**
- Anything else relevant to the financial outcome

**Examples:**
- Pre-nuptial or post-nuptial agreements
- Pending inheritance
- Health issues affecting future earning capacity
- Plans to relocate
- Immigration status issues

## Section 5: Order Sought

### 5.1: What You're Asking For

**Options:**
- **Periodical payments (maintenance)**: Ongoing regular payments
  - Joint lives (until death/remarriage)
  - Fixed term (e.g., 5 years)
  - Nominal (£1/year - keeps option open)

- **Lump sum**: One-off payment

- **Property adjustment**: Transfer of property or share of property

- **Pension sharing**: Percentage of pension transferred to your name

- **Clean break**: No ongoing financial claims between you

**Tips:**
- This is an indication, not a binding position
- Courts expect parties to negotiate from initial positions
- Get legal advice before committing to specific figures
- "To be determined" is acceptable initially

### 5.2: Statement of Truth

You must sign confirming:
- You understand the duty of full and frank disclosure
- The information is true to the best of your knowledge
- You understand proceedings for contempt of court may be brought if you make a false statement

**This is a legal declaration. False statements = perjury.**

## Document Checklist

### Essential Documents

| Document | Timeframe | Where to Get |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| Bank statements (all accounts) | 12 months | Online banking / branch |
| Payslips | 12 months | Employer / online portal |
| P60 | Latest | Employer |
| Tax returns | 2 years | HMRC online account |
| Property valuation | Within 6 months | Estate agents (get 2-3) |
| Mortgage statement | Current | Lender |
| Mortgage redemption figure | Current | Lender (shows early repayment charges) |
| Pension CETV | Within 12 months | Pension provider (request ASAP) |
| Business accounts | 2 years | Accountant |
| Investment valuations | Current | Provider statements |
| Life insurance values | Current | Provider |
| Credit card statements | 12 months | Provider |
| Loan statements | Current | Provider |
| Vehicle valuations | Current | Auto Trader / webuyanycar |

### Timeline for Document Gathering

**Immediately (Week 1):**
- Request pension CETVs (can take 3 months)
- Order Land Registry documents
- Request mortgage redemption figures

**Week 2-4:**
- Gather 12 months bank statements
- Collect payslips and P60
- Get property valuations

**Week 4-8:**
- Chase outstanding documents
- Get business accounts if applicable
- Obtain investment valuations

**Week 8-12:**
- Complete form
- Review and check
- Prepare document bundle

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Underestimating time needed** - Start gathering documents immediately
2. **Forgetting accounts** - Include ALL accounts, even small/dormant ones
3. **Outdated valuations** - Property values must be within 6 months
4. **Missing pension CETVs** - These take longest, request first
5. **Incomplete bank statements** - Every page, every account, 12 months
6. **Inconsistent figures** - Check totals match component parts
7. **Omitting debts** - Include all liabilities
8. **Inflated budgets** - Courts see many Forms E and spot unrealistic figures
9. **Conduct allegations** - Don't include unless truly exceptional and advised
10. **Unsigned/undated** - Form is invalid without Statement of Truth

## Tips for Success

### Be Thorough
- More detail is better than less
- Explain unusual transactions
- Provide context where helpful

### Be Honest
- Full disclosure is a legal duty
- Non-disclosure can invalidate any order made
- The other side will scrutinise everything

### Be Organised
- Number your documents
- Create a clear index
- Keep copies of everything

### Be Timely
- Start immediately
- Chase documents proactively
- Don't miss deadlines

### Get Help When Needed
- Complex businesses need accountant input
- Pensions may need actuary advice
- Legal advice is valuable for strategy

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| CETV | Cash Equivalent Transfer Value - the transfer value of a pension |
| FDA | First Directions Appointment - first court hearing |
| FDR | Financial Dispute Resolution - settlement hearing |
| Clean break | Order ending all financial claims between parties |
| Periodical payments | Regular maintenance payments |
| Lump sum | One-off capital payment |
| Pension sharing | Dividing pension so each has their own pot |
| Pension offsetting | Keeping pension intact but adjusting other assets |
| MPS | Maintenance Pending Suit - temporary maintenance during proceedings |
| Statement of Truth | Legal declaration that contents are true |
| Decree Nisi | Court order confirming entitlement to divorce |
| Decree Absolute | Final divorce order |
